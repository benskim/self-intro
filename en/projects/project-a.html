Overview Data analytics for service growth

Beta launch and user-acquisition (UA) growth project for a generative-AI B2C app

  • Context: Market launch of a mobile app offering face/voice generation AI built on images, audio, and video, together with the business data pipeline.
  • Target KPI: Reach PMF and maximize retention — build real-time user activation infrastructure.

Problem Definition Retention (CVR) bottleneck versus user inflow

Diagnosing the bottleneck between UA marketing spend and conversion

Problems on the ground

  • UA ads brought large inflow, but drop-off surged at the generative-AI experience step right after sign-up.
  • Among users who reached the video-source screen, the rate of actually generating an AI video stalled, delaying the aha-moment.
  • Marketing cost (CAC) kept rising while first-day churn after sign-up spiked.

Ad click & Install100%

Sign Up65%

AI Generation (drop-off hotspot)12%

Share/Download2.5%

Between in-app sign-up and the first AI generation attempt, more than 80% of users leak out.

Log Infrastructure User Behavior Log Architecture

User behavior log tracking architecture for real-time decision making

  • Collection and integration: Integrated a Firebase-based front-end action-log SDK and established unique user-ID mapping and sync.
  • Acquisition-source tracking: Designed Appsflyer parameters for ad-channel attribution and built the Amplitude cohort integration pipeline.
  • Core action-log schema:

app_open video_view face_swap face_swap_result_view share download like

EDA Identifying churn patterns through data exploration

Data-driven hypotheses on churn drivers from multidimensional user-log analysis

Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1 — When latency before the AI result exceeds 8 seconds, immediate user drop-out rises exponentially.

Hypothesis 2 — The higher the image-generation failure rate (face distortion), the faster customers churn, proportionally.

💡 SQL result: A user-segment analysis with SQL window functions showed that users who successfully received AI results twice had a 7-day retention rate 3.5x higher than users who had not.

Metric Definition & LTV Model Business Metric Optimization

Formulating lifetime value (LTV) and the North Star Metric

Metric formulation

  • Proxy LTV: \(\text{proxy LTV} = 1 / \text{GPU cost}\)
  • North Star Metric: ‘Number of users who download (or share) a generated AI result at least once within 24 hours of sign-up’

Unit economics growth strategy — Keep the proxy LTV/CAC ratio at 3.0 or higher to stabilize unit economics.

Hypothesis Testing A/B test scenario design

A practical A/B test scenario and statistical design for hypothesis testing

Segment Content hypothesis
Control (Group A) Existing random content (80% user-uploaded, 20% in-house)
Treatment (Group B) Genre-curated content (cinematic, Y2K, cartoon, meme, etc.)
  • Primary metric: Video conversion rate = share of users exposed to content who successfully generate an AI video (baseline 14.2%)
  • Assignment / duration: New users randomly assigned 50:50 with Firebase Remote Config (A/B Testing), branching the content-exposure policy value; 14 days (two weekly cycles). The group is logged as a user property and on action logs for per-group analysis in BigQuery and Amplitude.
  • Sample size: With significance level \(\alpha = 0.05\), power \(1-\beta = 0.80\), and a minimum detectable effect (MDE) of +6.5 pp (14.2% → 20.7%), at least 532 users per group, i.e. at least 38 users per segment per day over 14 days.

A/B Test Execution Statistical significance

Analyzing the A/B test and verifying significance at the 95% confidence level

Conversion proportions were tested with an independent-samples t-test and a chi-square test (576 users per group, about 41 per day).

Treatment (B) video conversion lift +5.6 pp (about +39%) A 14.2% → B 19.8% (82 → 114 of 576 per group)

Test statistic p = 0.012 < 0.05 95% CI of the difference: +1.2 to +9.9 pp

Conclusion: The difference was verified as statistically significant, not due to chance, and the new service scenario was decided for full rollout. The observed difference (+5.6 pp) is smaller than the design MDE (+6.5 pp), so power is about 71%; the effect size will be re-checked through post-rollout monitoring.

A detailed reproduction is in Report: A/B Test Analysis Note.

Tableau Real-time business growth dashboard

Real-time Tableau dashboard architecture to speed up business decisions

  • Dashboard spec — data sources: Real-time join of refined BigQuery logs and Appsflyer marketing performance tables.
  • Key charts
    • Generations per session and dwell time by content category [adjustable period]
    • Funnel chart by acquisition cohort with drop-off alerts
    • Retention versus GPU cost monitoring by marketing channel

Quantitative impact of data-driven business improvement
